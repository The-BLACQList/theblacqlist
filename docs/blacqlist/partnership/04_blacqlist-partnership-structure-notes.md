# Partnership Structure Notes: Discussion Starters

Read this first. This file is not legal advice and not financial advice. It does not set up a business, split ownership, or create any agreement. It is a list of things for the two of you to decide together, plus clear flags for when to bring in a professional. The point is to have an honest conversation now and put the important parts in writing before money and time pile up.

When you are ready to make anything official, a Georgia business attorney and a CPA are the right people to take this to. Nothing below replaces them.

---

## Why have this conversation now

Partnerships rarely break over the work. They break over things that were never said out loud. Who decides what. Who owns how much. What happens if someone wants out. It is far easier and far kinder to talk about these while everything is good and the stakes are still small. Doing it now is a sign the partnership is serious, not a sign of distrust.

---

## What to decide together

Use these as prompts for a real sit-down. There are no right answers here, only the answers that are right for the two of you.

### Roles and decision rights
- [ ] Confirm the lane split from the role design. Ebony owns field, community, ops, and in-person voice. Chalece owns product, build, brand system, and the technology.
- [ ] Decide what each of you can decide alone, and what needs both of you. A simple rule of thumb: small and reversible can be solo, big or hard to undo needs both.
- [ ] Name a tiebreaker approach for when you disagree and both feel strongly.

### Contribution and ownership
- [ ] Be honest about what each person is putting in. Chalece has put in the build, the product, and the brand so far. Ebony is putting in time, network, and on-the-ground work.
- [ ] Talk through how ownership should reflect that. This is the sweat-equity conversation. Flag for a professional before you land on any number.
- [ ] Consider whether ownership should be earned over time rather than granted all at once. This is the vesting idea. It protects both of you if life changes. Flag for a professional.

### Time and expectations
- [ ] Set a realistic expectation for Ebony's time that respects football. The role ramps with her offseason. Put that understanding in writing so no one feels let down later.
- [ ] Agree on what counts as meeting the commitment, so effort is judged fairly and not by vibes.

### Money
- [ ] Agree on how any early revenue is handled and reinvested while the platform is young.
- [ ] Decide if and when either partner takes pay, and keep that separate from ownership.
- [ ] Keep business money and personal money clearly apart from the start. This matters for taxes and for trust. Flag for a CPA.

### If things change
- [ ] Talk through what happens if one of you wants to step back or leave. Better to design the exit while you are friends than to negotiate it while you are not.
- [ ] Decide what happens to ownership and responsibilities in that case.

## What to put in writing eventually

You do not need all of this today. This is the checklist for when you formalize, with a professional's help.

- [ ] The legal form of the business and how it is registered. Flag for an attorney.
- [ ] Each partner's ownership share. Flag for an attorney and CPA.
- [ ] Whether ownership vests over time, and on what schedule. Flag for an attorney.
- [ ] Roles, decision rights, and the tiebreaker.
- [ ] How profits, losses, and any pay are handled. Flag for a CPA.
- [ ] What happens if a partner leaves, including how their share is handled.
- [ ] How the agreement itself can be changed later.

A written founder agreement is the goal. It does not have to be long. It has to be clear and signed.

## When to bring in a professional

Bring in the right professional before you commit to any of these, not after.

| Question | Who to ask |
|---|---|
| How to register the business and structure ownership | Georgia business attorney |
| Drafting the founder or operating agreement | Georgia business attorney |
| How to split profits, handle pay, and keep taxes clean | CPA or tax professional |
| Keeping business and personal finances separate | CPA |

---

## Privacy and money guardrails

- Keep sensitive details out of any shared document. No account numbers, no full financial figures, no personal identifiers. Categories and plain descriptions only.
- Do not co-mingle personal and business money. Even before there is much money, start the habit clean.
- Until the structure is formal and in writing, treat ownership and pay as open questions, not settled facts.
- Anything that touches legal status, ownership percentages, or taxes goes to a professional first.

## The simple version

Have the honest talk now, while it is easy. Write down the parts that matter. Take the legal and money pieces to people who do that for a living. Then get back to building the thing.
